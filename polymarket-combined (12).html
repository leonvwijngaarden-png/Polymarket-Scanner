Edge types explained
⚡ Arb gap
YES + NO don't sum to $1. Buy all outcomes for less than $1 guaranteed payout. Purely mathematical — no prediction needed.
💧 Low liquidity
Markets under $100k volume where a single whale can move price. Wide spreads = common mispricing.
🎯 Longshot bias
Outcomes priced 1–10% are historically ~35% overpriced. Buy NO on cheap longshots.
📈 Momentum
Price moved sharply on low volume. Likely a whale move — mean reversion candidate. "Fade" means bet against the move.
🌡 Temperature gap
ECMWF/NOAA forecast disagrees with Polymarket's implied probability for a city's daily high.
🌧 Rain gap
Forecast rain probability diverges from Polymarket's YES price by more than 15%.
How to read signals
EV % — expected value per dollar
If YES trades at 35¢ but true probability is 50%, EV = (0.50 × $1) − $0.35 = +$0.15 per share = +43% EV. The scanner surfaces the gap — whether the probability estimate is right is your judgment call.
Volume threshold matters
Markets under $100k volume are only 61% accurate — barely above a coin flip. Above $100k, accuracy climbs above 90%. Low-volume signals = higher risk, higher potential edge.
Half-Kelly sizing
Full Kelly on a 58% edge at 54¢ = ~8.5% of bankroll. Half-Kelly = 4.25%. Cuts variance roughly in half while keeping most of the expected return. Never more than 5% on any single market.
Arb gap — safest edge
YES = 40¢, NO = 55¢ → sum = 95¢. Buy both for 95¢, collect $1 at resolution. +5.3% guaranteed regardless of outcome. Move fast — liquid markets close these in minutes.
Resolution criteria edge
The market title and the resolution rules are often different. Traders price the headline; sharp traders price the resolution criteria. Always read the full market description before trading any signal.
