What this app does
How it works
The scanner fetches the ECMWF weather forecast for each city and compares it to the prices on Polymarket's daily temperature markets. If the forecast says 29°C but the market prices 29°C at only 30¢ (meaning traders think it's only 30% likely), that's a potential edge. You buy the bucket the forecast lands in and hope the forecast is right.
Where does the forecast come from?
US cities use the National Weather Service (NWS) — official NOAA observations from the exact airport station Polymarket uses. International cities use ECMWF via Open-Meteo — the same European model that professional traders use. The scanner also fetches 50 ensemble model runs to measure how much the models disagree.
How does Polymarket resolve temperatures?
Each market resolves to the highest temperature recorded at a specific airport weather station on that day. US markets use NWS data in Fahrenheit. International markets (London, Tokyo etc) use Wunderground station data in whole degrees Celsius, floored not rounded — so 29.7°C resolves as 29°C. The scanner accounts for this.
Signal types
⚡ Guaranteed arb (green border)
The combined cost of 3+ adjacent buckets is under 100¢. Since exactly one bucket MUST resolve YES (they cover a contiguous temperature range), buying all of them is risk-free profit. Example: three buckets cost 89¢ total — one resolves at $1.00 — guaranteed +11¢ profit regardless of the exact temperature. Move fast, these close within minutes.
🎯 Core-3 +EV bracket (amber border)
The combined cost is between 100–110¢ — not guaranteed profit, but positive expected value if the forecast is roughly correct. You buy the forecast bucket plus one either side, so a 1-bucket forecast error still wins. The forecast has to be in the right ballpark, not perfect.
🌡 Single bucket (blue border)
Just the exact bucket the forecast lands in. Higher EV per dollar than a bracket, but zero error tolerance — if the temperature comes in 1°C higher or lower than forecast, you lose everything. Only makes sense when the forecast is very confident (small ensemble spread, low model error, today's forecast).
Reading a signal card
Day badge and error margin
The coloured badge shows how far ahead the forecast is — Today, +1 day, +2 days, grey for +3 days. Next to it is the error margin: ±1.4°C ensemble means the 50 model runs span about 2.8°C total. ±2°F (no ensemble) is a fixed estimate for US cities. Smaller margin = more reliable forecast.
Model accuracy bar
The four-column bar under the forecast shows how accurate ECMWF has been for that city over the last 14 days. Avg error is the typical miss size. Bias tells you if the model runs consistently warm or cold — if it says +1.8°C warm, mentally shift the forecast down by 1.8°C and target the bucket below. Within 1°C is how often the model lands in the right bucket.
Bucket table — Buy $ and Shares
Each bucket has a different price, so to buy the same number of shares in each you spend different dollar amounts. The table shows exactly what to spend per bucket. When one bucket resolves YES it pays $1 per share — your total payout is Shares × $1. The → arrow marks which bucket the forecast lands in.
EV % — what it means
Expected value per dollar. If three buckets cost 89¢ combined and one must resolve at $1.00, EV = (1.00 − 0.89) / 0.89 = +12% guaranteed. For +EV brackets, EV is calculated assuming the forecast is correct — it's not guaranteed, it's the average return if the forecast is right.
Position sizing
Kelly criterion
The app uses the Kelly criterion to calculate how much to bet. Full Kelly maximises long-run growth but can mean huge swings. Quarter-Kelly (0.25) is the default — it gives about 75% of the growth rate with much lower variance. Set your bankroll and Kelly fraction in Settings; the sizing table updates automatically.
For guaranteed arbs: just buy them
Kelly sizing doesn't really apply to guaranteed arbs — any positive amount is correct since there's no risk. The app still shows a Kelly-sized suggestion as a guide, but you can deploy as much as the market's liquidity allows. Check the order book depth before placing large orders.
Forecasts tab
What the Forecasts tab shows
After scanning, the Forecasts tab shows the raw data for each city: today's temperature, the 5-day forecast strip, and for international cities — the ensemble spread (how much the 50 models disagree per day, shown in amber) and the ECMWF accuracy block showing the last 14 days of actual vs forecast with error per day.
